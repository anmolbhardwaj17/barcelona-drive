# v3 audit — Asset pipeline, material system, LOD architecture (cross-cutting foundation)
**VERDICT: REFACTOR**
## Justification
Direct answer to the rule-hard question: **the per-tile streaming + worker + merge pipeline is FIT for a textured world and rebuilding it would make the end result WORSE. The LOD layer inside it is not fit and gets replaced. The asset/material layer is not a rebuild because it does not exist — it is greenfield.**

Why not REBUILD the tile pipeline, argued on end-result grounds and not on sunk cost:

1. Texture residency is city-wide, not per-tile. A shared KTX2 library is loaded once and referenced by every tile. Textures therefore impose ZERO new requirement on tile granularity, tile format, or the streaming schedule. There is no version of "the tiles are wrong for textures" that survives contact with the data.
2. 500 m sectors streamed in a radius around the camera IS the ETS2 architecture (their .base/.aux sector grid). We would rebuild toward what we already have.
3. The one thing a rebuild is usually needed for — collapsing per-instance variation out of material identity so the material count stays bounded — **already landed** (`buildingWorker.js:381-392`, colour → vertex colours, material key → per-category). That was the expensive structural change and it is done. A textured facade sheet is now `map` + `normalMap` on 16 existing materials. Rebuilding would re-derive this conclusion at a cost of ~20 days.
4. Those ~20 days are the whole argument. Under the ETS2 calibration the bar is reached by COVERAGE — enough surfaces normal-mapped, enough props textured, enough of the city converted that there is no seam. Twenty days spent re-arriving at the current architecture is twenty days of coverage not shipped, and coverage is precisely what "better than what we have" means. A rebuild here is slacking in the direction the brief warns about second.

Why REFACTOR is not a soft answer — three things get genuinely torn out, not patched:

- **The LOD layer is REBUILT, not tuned.** Per-tile `nearEdgeDist` at 500 m granularity cannot be made to work by moving constants; `BUILDING_MAX_DISTANCE` is already 250 and every loaded tile is fully detailed regardless. It is replaced by per-object BatchedMesh pools with discrete distance tiers — ETS2's model. This is the item the whole city-wide GPU budget rests on (~3.0 ms) and the item that makes any art investment visible at the range where the player looks.
- **The material layer gets a chokepoint that does not exist today.** Ten sites hard-assign `onBeforeCompile` and clobber each other; two of them are copy-paste of the same injection. Every scene-wide v3 feature — IBL, detail-map, wet road, CSM — is blocked on this. `materialRegistry.js` is new code that takes ownership away from all ten.
- **The asset pipeline is written from nothing.** There is no KTX2, no manifest, no encode step, no byte ceilings, no colour-space or v-flip policy, no cache-immutability. "Refactor" understates this half; it is a build.

The honest one-word verdict for the domain as a whole is REFACTOR because the highest-cost, highest-risk component — tile streaming and worker geometry generation — is kept deliberately, and because `vegPools.js` already contains the hardened BatchedMesh machinery the LOD rebuild needs, which converts the riskiest item from greenfield into a generalization of proven code.
## ETS2 reference
ETS2 (SCS "Prism3D") at the "not too good but better than ours" calibration, subsystem by subsystem.

TEXTURE PIPELINE. Ships DDS with mips: BC1 (DXT1) for opaque, BC3 (DXT5) where alpha is needed, wrapped in a tiny .tobj descriptor that carries the sampler state (wrap, filter, anisotropy, sRGB flag) SEPARATELY from the image. Typical world tiling surface is 512x512; hero surfaces and facade sheets 1024x1024; 2048 is reserved for vehicle exteriors and a handful of landmarks. That is the number to copy — 512/1K, not 2K/4K. Everything is block-compressed at rest AND in VRAM; there is no uncompressed RGBA8 anywhere in the world render.

MAP SETS. Pre-2019 ETS2 is specular-gloss, not metal-rough: albedo (.dif), a GRAYSCALE specular mask usually packed into the albedo's alpha channel, and a tangent-space normal (.nmap) only on surfaces where it reads — asphalt, kerbs, brick, guardrail, terrain. Large flat painted surfaces often have NO normal map at all. There is no per-texture AO map; AO is baked into albedo and into vertex colours. There is no roughness map, no parallax, no detail-normal blending. A shared low-frequency detail/grunge texture is multiplied over surfaces at a second UV scale to break tiling repetition — this is the single trick that does the most work in their look and it costs one 512 single-channel texture city-wide.

MATERIAL SYSTEM. Materials are declarative .mat files naming one of a FIXED, small set of shader effects (eut2.dif, eut2.dif.spec, eut2.dif.spec.nmap, eut2.building, eut2.lamp, eut2.truckpaint, ...). Variation between two hundred buildings is bought with per-instance vertex colour tint and UV offset into a shared facade sheet — NOT with a new material. The engine's material count is bounded by effect-count, not by asset-count. This is the load-bearing structural fact for us.

GEOMETRY + BATCHING. Cities are assembled from a KIT of prefab building models, each a few hundred to ~2000 triangles carrying 1-2 materials, UV-mapped into a shared facade atlas cell with padding. Static geometry inside a sector is batched per material. Vegetation and repeated props are hardware-instanced.

LOD. Discrete, AUTHORED, per-model: each .pmg contains an explicit LOD chain (lod1/lod2/lod3) with distance thresholds in the model descriptor plus a hard cull distance. Vegetation ends its chain in a camera-facing billboard from a shared atlas. There is no continuous LOD and no geomorphing — pops are masked by fog and by distance. Critically the switch is PER MODEL, not per sector.

STREAMING. The world is a grid of sectors (.base/.aux/.data) loaded in a radius around the camera, with the LOD chain evaluated per model inside the loaded sectors. Our per-tile streaming IS this architecture. Our LOD is not.

Net: ETS2's foundation = block-compressed 512/1K albedo+normal+spec-mask, a bounded effect set, per-instance tint instead of per-instance material, prefab kits batched per material, and per-MODEL discrete LOD chains inside a sector stream. We already have the streaming and (as of the vertex-colour collapse) the bounded material set. We have none of the compression, none of the maps, and none of the per-model LOD.

## Risks
- HIGHEST RISK — BatchedMesh geometry-buffer fragmentation. Buildings, unlike trees, have UNIQUE geometry per instance, so the LOD pools must addGeometry/deleteGeometry per building on every tile stream. three's _reserveRange reuses freed ranges only when the new geometry fits, so a long drive fragments the pool and eventually throws 'Maximum item count reached' or silently drops buildings. vegPools.js:56-61 documents the sibling landmine (deleteInstance's O(n log n) freed-list sort) but does NOT cover deleteGeometry. MITIGATION: size pools from the measured p99 tile (GLOBAL_VERTEX_BUDGET 100000 x 9 tiles = 900k verts worst case), bucket by vertex-count class so freed ranges are reusable, and run the 10-minute heap-growth loop from gate item 9 specifically against fragmentation. If it fails, fall back to the 1-day per-mesh-distance item, which recovers ~1.0 of the 3.0 ms.
- The warm-list ordering is a one-shot trap. The moment the first material gains map/normalMap/aoMap, the define set changes and all 125 compiled programs invalidate simultaneously — the recorded symptom is one-off ~100 ms frames (meshMaterializer.js:965-970). If the extended warm list lands AFTER the first textured material rather than with it, the corridor gate's p95 frame time measures a compile storm and reads as a fidelity failure. Sequencing, not engineering.
- Silent-failure cluster in the encode pipeline. Three independent failure modes produce NO error and NO warning: (a) wrong KTX2 colour-space flag — three reads transfer straight off the DFD and verifyColorSpace early-returns for compressed textures, so an sRGB normal map just looks subtly wrong; (b) v-flip — WebGL ignores UNPACK_FLIP_Y_WEBGL for compressed uploads, so every .ktx2 renders un-flipped against UVs authored for flipY canvas textures, which reads as 'the texture looks off' not 'the texture is upside down' on tiling surfaces; (c) missing FORMAT_OPTIONS override — Windows silently gets 8bpp BC7 for ETC1S content, doubling VRAM invisibly on the platform we cannot test locally. All three must be asserted in the build script with non-zero exits, not caught by eye.
- The corridor gate measures a moving target. Task #39 (frame pipeline, worst-frame 23.9-25.4 ms of untagged `other` during stream-in) is the exact regime the per-object LOD rebuild also touches, and adaptiveResolution reads CPU stalls as GPU load and blurs the screen (adaptiveResolution.js:23, FAST_MS = 15.5 is below the 16.67 ms vsync floor, so on 60 Hz scale can only ratchet down). If those two land concurrently, neither can be attributed. Sequence #39 and the adaptive re-gate ahead of the LOD rebuild or accept uninterpretable numbers.
- The disposal inversion touches 13 sites across 6 files and a wrong tag is a black-texture or a crash, not a warning. Ship it with a dev-mode assert that walks the registry after every tile unload and throws if any registry-owned material or texture has been disposed. This is the one P0 that must not be deferred: it is harmless today and catastrophic the day a shared KTX2 texture exists.
- CC0 licence provenance is not currently tracked anywhere. frontend/public/textures/new textures/ contains two craftpix-* directories (now empty but for .DS_Store) — CraftPix 'free' licences are NOT CC0 and restrict redistribution. Before the library grows, the manifest must carry a per-asset source + licence field and the build script must fail on a missing or non-CC0 licence. drive.anmolbhardwaj.com is a live custom domain, so this is a real exposure, not hygiene.
- Time-to-drive is unmeasured and structurally invisible. main.js:631-635 holds the loader until isInitialLoadComplete() with a 20 s safety net at :723, so a serious regression from a blocking hero asset set surfaces only when it is severe. Zero-install click-and-drive is binding constraint 1 and nobody is watching the metric that expresses it.
- GLOBAL_VERTEX_BUDGET drops entire buildings (buildingWorker.js:1098-1101). Every art-side geometry addition — parapets, 24-vertex boxes, roof detail — silently deletes buildings in exactly the dense Eixample tiles the gate measures.
