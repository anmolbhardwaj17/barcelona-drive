# v3 audit — Terrain, hills, beach, coastline (terrainRenderer.js, coastline.js, areaFeaturesRenderer.js, vegetationMask.js, fastElevation.js, backend/worldBuilder/terrainBaker.js)
**VERDICT: REBUILD**
## Justification
Rebuild of the SURFACING and the DELIVERY; explicit KEEP of the data model. This is not a rewrite for its own sake — the two halves fail for different, independently sufficient reasons.

SURFACING cannot be patched to the ETS2 bar. ETS2 ground is per-vertex weight blending of tiling albedo+normal materials; ours is a 5-layer procedural FBM that OVERRIDES the CPU vertex colours at 75% (terrainRenderer.js:886) and is tuned as one closed system. You cannot bolt a splat texture on top of it — the two fight for the same pixel and the FBM wins by construction. Reaching the bar means deleting terrainRenderer.js:557-763 (the CPU colour pass) and :836-897 (the procedural block) and replacing the attribute set (colour/aCoast) with baked splat weights. ~600 lines out, ~200 in. That is a rebuild whether or not we call it one, so cost it as one. And the payoff is disproportionate: terrain is the largest continuous surface in every frame, and it is the surface with literally zero sub-5-metre detail today. This is the most visible single instance of the "flat-shaded untextured look" the user is complaining about.

DELIVERY is a different argument and it is the one that PAYS FOR the art. 369.0 MB of 542.6 MB (68.0%) of the tile corpus is bakedTerrain, and buildRegion.js:1571 proves it is a pure deterministic function of the 64 KB elevation grid shipped in the same file (all four topology arguments are empty, so hasTunnels is false and the index buffer is the plain grid). Another 14.4 MB is bakedPhysicsTerrain, parsed and handed to createTerrainTrimesh (tileManager.js:1179) which is never called. Deleting both takes the corpus to ~175 MB and the per-tile fetch from ~1.27 MB to ~0.41 MB. Nothing else in this project moves a number that large. Generating the mesh in the tile worker also lets us emit LOD index rings and drop the ignored normals/Uint32 indices, which is where the triangle headroom for a 1.5 km terrain silhouette comes from.

WHAT I AM NOT REBUILDING, and why sunk cost has nothing to do with it: the 128x128 DEM grid is correct. 3.61 m/cell matches the AO grid resolution (aoRes=128, verified on 4 tiles), matches the physics Heightfield (tileManager.js:1693, which reads the FULL source grid after the D-16 quadrant bug), and is the surface roads drape onto under D-16-target. Changing it would re-open a decision log entry and a gotcha (G-49) for no visual gain — the measured p90 DEM range is 70.5 m, so the hills are already faithfully described. Also KEEP: terrainBaker's carve/trench logic, the aoSampler integration, coastline.js's OSM stitcher, fastElevation.js, and the getElevationAt-must-match-the-rendered-ground contract (changelog 2026-07-05, the floating-buildings root cause).

The SEA is the one genuinely new subsystem. It is currently a vertex colour on a 2.05-5.78 m bumpy terrain plateau (measured, 16_33168_24478). No amount of colour tuning makes that read as water. It needs a real surface mesh and a bake-side sink, and coastline.js:12 already agreed with that conclusion before I got here.
## ETS2 reference
Prism3D terrain, at the "not too good but better than ours" calibration:

1. GEOMETRY: terrain is spline-attached strips plus freeform quads, hand-shaped by level designers. Vertex density is LOW and NON-UNIFORM — dense at the road shoulder where you see it, sparse in the field. 2-3 mesh LODs plus a separate distant "far model" so there is ALWAYS a terrain silhouette out to several km. Cliffs and rock faces are NOT terrain: they are modelled prop meshes with their own UVs, placed against the slope. No triplanar anywhere.

2. SURFACING (the thing we do not have at all): per-vertex material-weight painting, ~3-4 weights blended, over a library of TILING ground materials — grass, dry grass, dirt, gravel, sand, rock. Each material is roughly 512-1024 px albedo WITH A MATCHING NORMAL MAP, tiling at roughly 4-8 m. The repeat is visible from the cab; ETS2 does not hide it, it just breaks it with a low-frequency macro variation layer and with props (bushes, rocks, litter). This is the exact opposite of our approach: they have low-frequency variation FROM VERTEX WEIGHTS and high-frequency detail FROM TEXELS; we have low-frequency variation from noise and NO high-frequency detail at all.

3. TERRAIN-TO-ROAD BLEND: their grass creeps over the asphalt edge with a soft alpha skirt. It is the single cheapest ETS2 tell and we have a hard geometric edge.

4. WATER: a separate mesh, not terrain. Two scrolling normal maps at different rates, fresnel, horizon fade, cubemap/planar reflection. Shorelines get a foam decal strip.

5. LOD SCHEME: mesh LOD by distance plus material simplification (normal map dropped, blend count reduced) in the far band.

Deliberately NOT in scope at this calibration: virtual texturing, megatexture, tessellation, parallax occlusion, screen-space terrain AO, photoscanned ground. ETS2 has none of these.

## Risks
- RE-BAKE, and it must be ONE bake. Four of my items (delete bakedTerrain, sink the sea, bake splat weights, and the format v10 bump) all need the same re-bake, and so do the vegetation domain's tree species (v2 3.5, NUM_TREE_VARIANTS is baked Uint8), any AO grid extension (v2 3.4) and any tangent bake (v2 3.6). Four separate re-bakes would be four TILE_VERSION bumps and four `window._clearTileCache()` events for every live player. This needs a cross-domain bake freeze and a single v10 cut. Mitigating: the corpus SHRINKS 542.6 -> ~175 MB, so the re-commit is cheaper than any previous one.
- getElevationAt IS THE HIGHEST-RISK LINE IN THE PLAN. terrainRenderer.js:905-985 samples the BAKED MESH POSITIONS specifically because the raw grid used to lack tunnel carving and water dips — that fix (changelog 2026-07-05) is what stopped buildings, colliders, pedestrians and parked cars from floating. I have evidence the grid now carries both mutations (buildRegion.js:1178-1249 water sink, :1327 trench carve documented as the LAST grid mutation), but 'has evidence' is not 'proved'. Deleting the bake before the bit-equality harness passes reintroduces the single most expensive bug in this codebase's history. Hence the prove-then-delete ordering, and hence the assert stays in permanently.
- DOUBLE WATER DIP. terrainRenderer.js:225-260 applies a SECOND runtime water depression (MAX_DEPTH -2.0, DEPTH_ZONE 20) on the fallback path. Once the mesh is always generated at runtime, that path becomes the only path and the dip double-applies on top of the bake-side sink. It must be deleted in the same commit, not a follow-up.
- SEA SINK CHANGES PHYSICS. buildTerrainHeightfield (tileManager.js:1693) reads the FULL source grid, so sinking sea cells to -3.0 m means driving off the Passeig Maritim drops the car into a hole instead of onto a 3.57 m plateau. That is arguably correct, but it interacts with the out-of-bounds teleport and the wedge auto-recovery breadcrumb (changelog 2026-07-12) and needs a deliberate drive test at the seafront spawn.
- FLATSHADING REMOVAL CONTRADICTS THE SHIPPED ART DIRECTION. rallyStyle.js:8 makes art-of-rally the DEFAULT look, and terrainRenderer.js:774 faceted hills are part of it. ETS2 terrain is smooth-shaded. This is the one place where 'ETS2 target' and the current shipped direction are in direct conflict and it cannot be resolved by a subagent — it needs the user's eyes on a before/after Montjuic pose. If the answer is 'keep the facets', the splat shader still lands but reads noticeably more stylised than the reference.
- DataArrayTexture + KTX2 IN r183. Loading a 2D-array KTX2 into a THREE.DataArrayTexture via KTX2Loader is not a well-trodden path in r183. Fallbacks are 4 separate compressed textures (costs 4 more texture units and kills the array-index blend) or one 2048 atlas with manual mip-safe padding (the classic bleed-at-high-mips failure, which on tiling ground shows as coloured seams every 6 m). Needs a half-day spike BEFORE the 4-day shader item is scheduled.
- SHARED-MATERIAL DISPOSAL. tileManager.js:2868-2872 disposes material.map and the material unless userData.sharedMaterial is set, and the terrain material is allocated per tile (terrainRenderer.js:769). Today this is safe only by accident, because the detail texture sits in a custom uniform rather than in .map. The moment a splat array is attached conventionally, the first tile unload destroys the city-wide texture. v2 item 0.2 (invert the default to explicit ownedMaterial opt-in) is a hard prerequisite for my P2 shader, not a nice-to-have.
- SPLAT AUTHORING NEEDS A LAND-COVER SOURCE AND OSM'S IS UNEVEN. Barcelona's landuse coverage is good inside the city and thin over Collserola. Where it is absent the rule degrades to slope+elevation, which produces visible banding at the rule boundary. Mitigation: dither the rule threshold with the same value noise and always let the macro breakup layer cross the boundary.
- 17 OF 426 TILES ARE STILL v7 WITH NO AO GRID. Any new baked per-vertex attribute needs a null path with a neutral default, exactly as buildingWorker.js:1071 does for AO. A missing splat attribute must fall back to 'all weight on layer 0' and never to zero weights, or those tiles render black.
- THE 4 SEA TILES WITH NaN BAKED NORMALS (guarded at terrainRenderer.js:559-568) become moot once normals are generated at runtime — but the guard's REASON (degenerate flat triangles at the seam) will reappear in the generator unless the same sanitisation moves with it. NaN reaching the GPU renders WHITE, which is how it was found the first time.
- I AM CLAIMING A SUBSYSTEM THAT DOES NOT EXIST. There is no sea renderer today; the sea is a vertex colour in my file. If the synthesis assigns water to a different owner, my P1 sea sink and P2 sea mesh must move with it — but the sink is a bake change and the terrain must stop painting sea either way, so the two cannot be scheduled independently.

Someone must own a single v10 cut with a bake freeze; I cannot schedule P1 or P2 against an unknown bake date.
- MATERIALS / SHADING — blocking. My splat shader is written against whatever the terrain material ends up being. Lambert works (r183 Lambert takes normalMap and aoMap, per the brief), MeshStandard works better. But I own material.onBeforeCompile outright (terrainRenderer.js:781) and a customProgramCacheKey (:903), so if the materialRegistry chokepoint (v2 item 1.5) lands I must CHAIN rather than assign, and that has to be coordinated, not discovered.
- SHARED-MATERIAL DISPOSAL FIX (v2 item 0.2) — hard prerequisite for my P2 shader. Without the inverted default, the first tile unload disposes the city-wide splat array texture (tileManager.js:2868-2872).
- ART ENCODE PIPELINE (v2 item 2.3) — blocking for every texture I ask for. My normal maps fail SILENTLY if the KTX2 transfer function is sRGB instead of linear (three reads it straight off the DFD and verifyColorSpace early-returns for compressed textures), and every ground material renders upside-down against the terrain UVs if the v-flip decision goes the other way. Both are invisible in code review and obvious on screen.
- VEGETATION — data dependency, both directions. My splat land-cover classification and their tree/grass placement must read the SAME source, or the ground says bare_rock where a plane tree is planted. vegetationMask.js already rasterises roads/junctions/bridges/water at 0.5 m and is the natural place to share it.

- LIGHTING / NIGHT — my P4 wetness item is only worth doing if the night lighting plan lands. With zero punctual lights (main.js:192) a wet ground mask has nothing to specular against except the 2 headlight SpotLights, which is a real but narrow win.
- MOBILE QUALITY TIER (v2 item 3.7) — my P4 tier item assumes a mechanism that does not exist anywhere in the codebase today (the only device branching is three copies of (pointer: coarse) for HUD layout). Without it my 14 MiB VRAM ask is unconditional on every device.
