# v3 audit — Signage — billboards, shop fascias, street-name/direction boards, traffic signs, lane arrows, traffic lights
**VERDICT: REBUILD**
## Justification
REBUILD the rendering/resource layer; KEEP ~450 lines of placement logic verbatim; DELETE trafficLightRenderer.js. This is not a code-aesthetics rewrite — the current architecture is arithmetically incapable of reaching the bar. Three independent hard walls: (1) one MeshBasicMaterial per board means ~300 draws for direction boards alone against a 450-draw CITY budget; (2) one 0.750 MiB texture per unique street name with zero cache eviction means 8,966 named Barcelona roads cannot be signed at any VRAM budget; (3) tile unload disposes the module-cached texture because the per-board materials lack `userData.sharedMaterial`. No amount of patching fixes "a texture per string" — the only fix IS the rebuild (shared atlas + a bounded LRU text page + pooled instances), which is what ETS2 does and what shopSignRenderer.js ALREADY does correctly in miniature. So the rebuild target is a generalisation of code we already have working, not a leap. Conversely, applying the no-slacking rule in the OTHER direction: `findIntersections` (:192), `walkPolyline` (:112), `isOnAnyRoad` (:956), `groundInstances` (:1360), `generateSpeedSigns` (:724), `generateDirectionBoards` (:977), `generateGantries` (:1131) and `toCatalanTitleCase` (:584) are ~450 lines of correct, terrain-aware, road-clearance-checked, Catalan-correct placement logic. Rewriting them would be waste that steals budget another domain needs. They are KEPT unchanged. shopSignRenderer.js's architecture (one atlas, one InstancedMesh, per-instance aUvOffset, one draw per tile) is already RIGHT and becomes the template for the whole domain — only its content, bay alignment, filtering, night behaviour and culling change. trafficLightRenderer.js is DELETED outright: it duplicates a better implementation, is disabled, and is broken (Y=0, dead Lambert params). And the ruling the brief asked for: YES, traffic lights should be rebuilt — but inside roadInfraRenderer, seeded from the 807 unused OSM `trafficSignals` nodes rather than guessed junction geometry, which structurally removes the "ugly box in the driving path" failure that got them killed. Finally, signage is the single highest-value-per-MiB subsystem available to us right now, because with ZERO punctual lights at night (main.js:192) an emissive fascia/pharmacy-cross/shop-sign layer is the ONLY way to put believable light sources in the night street without adding a single light to the scene.
## ETS2 reference
ETS2 signage is a SIGN ATLAS + INSTANCED PLATES system, not per-sign textures. Concretely, at the "not too good but better than ours" calibration: (1) Regulatory signs (speed discs, STOP, ceda-el-paso, no-entry) are ~128-256px cells inside one shared 1-2K albedo atlas, alpha-cutout, mounted on a shared 8-tri galvanised post. One albedo map only — no normal map on a 0.7m flat printed disc; the wear is painted into the albedo. (2) Direction/gantry boards are a BACKPLATE quad from the atlas + separate TEXT quads sampled from a font/decal page, composited in world space — that is how they get arbitrary city names without a texture per string. (3) Sign LETTERING IS DELIBERATELY OVERSIZED relative to real regulation (Spanish motorway lettering is 200-350mm, which at 100m subtends 1.9-3.3px at 1080p/60deg — unreadable); ETS2 runs roughly 2x regulation cap-height so the sign reads at 80-150m, which is the actual game-feel requirement. (4) Shop fascias are per-BAY, not per-building: a modular shopfront kit repeats every ~3-4m along a ground floor, each bay getting a fascia box (face + two side returns, 3 quads, NOT a floating plane) with a category-appropriate printed sign. (5) Night is where ETS2 buys the most: fascias, pharmacy crosses and fuel-station totems carry an emissive channel, and regulatory plates use a retroreflective response to headlights. (6) Traffic signals at every urban junction with a modelled 3-aspect head and a black backboard — an urban grid with zero signals is one of the loudest "tech demo" tells. (7) LOD is UV-swap + drop: plates keep drawing to ~180m, text quads are simply dropped past readable range (~70-90m) and the backplate reads as a coloured shape. Total signage texture budget in a game like this is single-digit MiB; the value-per-MiB is the highest of any art subsystem because signage is what makes a world read as an inhabited PLACE rather than geometry.

## Risks
- THE X-MIRROR TRAP — highest-probability silent breakage. worldGroup.scale.x = -1 is compensated three incompatible ways today: tex.repeat.x=-1 + offset.x=1 (roadInfraRenderer.js:302-303, 510-511, 701-702) and a shader 1.0-uv.x (shopSignRenderer.js:90-95). repeat/offset are per-TEXTURE and therefore destroy per-cell atlas offsets. The rebuild MUST standardise on the shader flip and the atlas material must assert repeat.x === 1. Get this wrong and every sign mirrors its text with no error.
- KTX2 DEPENDENCY IS HARD. No KTX2Loader exists in frontend/src and public/basis/ does not exist. A PNG-fallback 2048x2048 RGBA8+mips atlas is 21.8 MiB — 2.4x my entire VRAM ask. If KTX2 slips, the atlas must drop to 1024x1024 (5.5 MiB PNG) and the regulatory cells shrink to 64px, which costs legibility. Do not ship the PNG fallback at 2048.
- R8 DYNAMIC-ATLAS MIP REGENERATION. copyTextureToTexture writes level 0 only; WebGL2 generateMipmap regenerates the WHOLE 2048x1024 chain (~0.3 ms) on every cell change. In dense Eixample a fast drive can dirty many cells per second. Mitigation: throttle regeneration to <=2/s and coalesce dirty cells; fallback is clamping text sampling to mip 0-2 and accepting mild aliasing beyond 40 m (still strictly better than today's zero mips).
- LRU THRASH. p90 is 97 shops/tile and ~12 tiles are resident, so up to ~1,164 candidate strings compete for 128 text cells. Mitigation: only bays within ~90 m get real text; beyond that the category backplate draws with no text quad. This is exactly what ETS2 does and it must be designed in from cell one, not bolted on.
- RE-OPENING THE ORIGINAL COMPLAINT. Traffic lights and roadside boards were removed because they read as ugly floating poles in the driving path (commit 05231ae). Restoring them without the OSM-node placement, the isOnAnyRoad clearance assert (roadInfraRenderer.js:956) and the groundInstances terrain-snap (:1360) will reproduce the exact failure and get them deleted a second time. Each restore ships with a screenshot gate.
- ART-DIRECTION COHESION. Signs are legitimately the most saturated objects in a real street, but if they are not palette-snapped through the same normalize pass as every other texture (v2 item 2.3) they will read as stickers pasted onto a muted world. Signage must be an explicit exception list in that pipeline, not an oversight.
- FONT LICENSING. The site is deployed on a custom domain. Only OFL/Apache fonts. A DIN or Transport clone with a restrictive EULA is a legal problem, not a style problem.
- TRADEMARK. Billboard and fascia artwork must be fictional. Do not AI-generate anything that reproduces a real brand mark, even 'in the style of' — the site is publicly deployed.
- SHARED-MATERIAL DISPOSAL. Until tileManager.js:2856-2872 is inverted to opt-in ownership (pipeline v2 item 0.2), a single missed userData.sharedMaterial on any signage mesh disposes the CITY-WIDE atlas on one tile unload and kills all signage for the session. My atlas is exactly the asset that bug was predicted to destroy.
- SCOPE HONESTY. 22.5 days is the full domain. If it must be cut, the ordered value is: P0 data wiring + P2 shop fascias (real OSM names on the bay grid) delivers roughly 60% of the perceived gain for ~7 days; gantries and billboards are the tail.
