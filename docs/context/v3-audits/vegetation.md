# v3 audit — Vegetation — trees, bushes, grass, parks/greens
**VERDICT: REBUILD**
## Justification
REBUILD the vegetation ART path; explicitly KEEP `vegPools.js` and the worker instancing plumbing.

Why the art path cannot be patched to the bar: an untextured `IcosahedronGeometry(detail 0)` has 20 faces. There is no amount of colour tuning, wind, AO, or lighting that gives a 20-face convex solid a tree silhouette. ETS2's entire tree look is silhouette — alpha-cut leaf edges against the sky — and a convex blob has no alpha edge to cut. Every knob on the current material has already been turned (three separate palette re-grades are recorded in the comments at `vegetationRenderer.js:62-70` and `vegetationWorker.js:1616-1623`, plus a night x1.55 restore and a billboard night tint that is itself annotated as having overshot twice). That is a subsystem that has been sanded to its ceiling. The ceiling is below the bar.

Why REBUILD is cheap rather than expensive here, and why it is not a rewrite for its own sake:
- The hard part is already built and is genuinely good. `vegPools.js` — fixed-capacity BatchedMesh sets, own LIFO free list to keep `addInstance` off its O(n log n) sort path, sibling-pool overflow, nearest-first id ordering for count-fade, wash in the unused colour alpha — is the product of real measured pain (the comments cite 60-110 ms realloc stalls and multi-second streaming degradation). A card tree is `geometries: [24 card geometries]` instead of `[4 blob geometries]` in the SAME `createVegPoolSet` call at `meshMaterializer.js:1053-1058`. `OBJLoader` emits non-indexed `{position, normal, uv}`, matching the pool's existing non-indexed layout on 3 of 4 attributes; the only gap is the `color` attribute, which must be kept and neutralised to white because `patchVegWash` rides on its alpha.
- The rebuild REDUCES triangles. Card trees are 2.1x per tree, but the per-instance LOD ladder they enable takes 851,743 tris down to ~300,000. The rebuild hands ~540k triangles back to the buildings and props domains.
- The single biggest quality delta in the whole domain costs 0.5 days and no re-bake: stop throwing away `result.trees`. 35,580 real, positioned, partly species-tagged Barcelona street trees are being parsed and dropped at `tileManager.js:1469` right now. "Species variety by context" is item 3.5 in the v2 plan, deferred there as REQUIRING A FULL RE-BAKE. That conclusion is wrong. The re-bake is only needed if you insist on the baked `Uint8` variant byte; the worker already receives `tileData.greens` and `tileData.roads` (`tileManager.js:1863`) and can re-derive species from real OSM species + polygon context at runtime, ignoring the baked byte entirely. NO RE-BAKE ANYWHERE IN THIS PLAN.

What gets DELETED outright, not rebuilt: `bushRenderer.js` (344 lines, zero importers), `zoneVegetationRenderer.js` (587 lines, imported and never called), `grassRenderer.js` (748 lines, gated to zero since July). 1,679 lines of code that produce nothing. Also the hand-drawn `ctx.ellipse` billboard atlas and, once LOD0 shadow casting lands, the 13,963-instance transparent blob-shadow pool.

Sunk cost is not an argument, and neither is novelty: the two files I am not touching (`vegPools.js`, the worker's instance-matrix builders) are not preserved because they exist, they are preserved because rewriting them would reintroduce four documented streaming stalls for zero visual gain.
## ETS2 reference
ETS2 (and ATS on the same Prism3D vegetation path) does five concrete things, all of which are reachable in a browser and none of which are photoreal:

1. GEOMETRY: cross-plane / cluster-card trees. A short solid trunk (8-12 tris, cylinder with a real bark strip in the atlas) plus 8-20 alpha-tested quads carrying leaf-cluster cutouts, oriented in a rough cross/fan so the silhouette holds from any yaw. Typical LOD0 is 100-250 tris. NOT a solid mesh, NOT per-leaf geometry. Our 8 unused .obj files measure 115-183 tris (tree_01=115, tree_02=147, tree_03=130, tree_04=117, tree_05=171, tree_06=182, tree_07=166, tree_08=183) — dead centre of the ETS2 band.

2. TEXTURES: one shared atlas per "climate set", ~2048x1024 or 2048x2048, holding 4-8 species in a grid, each cell 512x512, with a bark strip occupying the bottom band of each cell. Maps: albedo+opacity in one RGBA, plus a tangent-space normal. No roughness/metalness on foliage — it is not worth the sample. Anisotropic filtering + mips are mandatory (unmipped foliage is the classic shimmer failure).

3. LOD: 3 tiers, hard-swapped with hysteresis, per-INSTANCE not per-tile. LOD0 full card set (~150 tris) to ~50-70 m; LOD1 the same trunk with the small filler cards deleted (~40 tris) to ~150-200 m; LOD2 a single camera-facing impostor quad (2 tris) rendered offline from the LOD0 model, out to fog. The impostor is a RENDER of the real asset, not a hand-drawn shape — that is why ETS2's distance treeline reads correctly.

4. SPECIES BY CONTEXT, not by hash. Roadside rows use one or two "avenue" species at regular ~7-10 m spacing; woodland masses use a different set at irregular density; the transition is driven by the map data (road class, land-use polygon), not by a random variant index. This is the single loudest ETS2 cue and it is nearly free.

5. GROUND COVER: parks/verges are a TILING GRASS TEXTURE (albedo+normal, ~1K, 2-4 m repeat) on the terrain, and grass geometry is a narrow band of 2-4 tri cards inside ~30-40 m of the camera along the road corridor only. ETS2 does not instance grass across a whole park. Roadside is a grass strip texture plus a thin card band, with wind on the cards.

Calibration check against "not too good but definitely better than ours": ETS2 trees visibly repeat, their leaf cards visibly pop at LOD swaps, and their bark is a 512-wide strip. That is the bar. It is roughly 2.3x our triangle count per tree and infinitely more than our zero textures — but it is not Quixel and does not need to be.
