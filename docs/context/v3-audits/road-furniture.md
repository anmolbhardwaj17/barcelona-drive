## Justification
The load-bearing fact is C1: I parsed all 426 baked Barcelona tiles and the OSM barrier layer contains SEVEN `guard_rail` features and THREE `jersey_barrier` features city-wide, against 1,120 walls / 895 fences / 390 compound_walls. Turning ENABLE_BARRIERS back on delivers garden walls and hedges, not the "railing on roads" the user asked for. No amount of patching barrierRenderer.js reaches the ETS2 bar because the DATA SOURCE IS EMPTY. Road furniture must be DERIVED from road class + geometry + terrain, which is a different program from what barrierRenderer.js is. Second: 57% of Barcelona tiles (243 of 426) contain zero elevated road, and the only railing gate we have is `isElevatedGuardRailRoad` — so in the majority of the city there is currently no railing at all, and that is structural, not a bug to fix. Third: both live railing materials are MeshBasicMaterial (`roadRenderer.js:2766,2773`), i.e. unlit. The single most ETS2 thing about a guardrail at night is headlights raking across corrugated galvanized steel; an unlit flat-colour box beam can never do that no matter how it is tuned. That is a rebuild of the material AND the geometry (a W-profile, not a box). What I am NOT rebuilding, deliberately: `computeGuardRailMask` (`roadRenderer.js:3246-3376`) is genuinely good work — junction/endpoint/roundabout/parallel-dedup suppression shared by geometry and colliders so they cannot drift. Keep and extend it; do not rewrite it. Net: REBUILD the emitters, materials, and placement gates; DELETE 1,243 lines of Delhi legacy (crashBarrierRenderer 451 + dividerRenderer 142 + reflectorRenderer 329 + barrierRenderer precast/gate blocks ~321 of the 650); KEEP the mask and the collider-mirrors-visual contract.
## ETS2 reference
ETS2's road-furniture is a small modular kit, reused everywhere, at honest mid-fidelity — NOT photoscanned. Concretely, at the "not too good but better than ours" calibration: (1) W-BEAM GUARDRAIL ("bionda") is the single strongest ETS2 signature — a swept W-profile ribbon (8-10 point cross-section, ~0.31 m tall corrugation) on IPN/sigma posts at 2-4 m spacing, on a 1K galvanized-steel albedo + 1K normal (the corrugation highlight is what sells it; the mesh itself is ~4 tris/m). It appears on EVERY at-grade shoulder with a drop-off, not just bridges. (2) CABLE GUARDRAIL on rural sections — 3 wire rope runs on the same post kit. (3) NEW JERSEY CONCRETE BARRIER for medians and roadworks — extruded profile, ~0.9-1.0 m, 1K weathered concrete albedo + normal with a dirt gradient at the base; instanced in ~4 m modules with visible joint lines. (4) DELINEATOR POSTS ("hitos"/captafaros) — white plastic post, 1.0-1.2 m, red and white reflective bands, at 50 m spacing; these are the elements that CATCH HEADLIGHTS, done with a high-specular/emissive-masked strip rather than real retroreflection. (5) BRIDGE PARAPETS — concrete kerb wall with a tubular steel railing (2 horizontal rails + posts), textured, not a solid slab. LOD scheme: rail ribbon geometry is not LOD'd (it is already ~4 tris/m); the POSTS are what get dropped, at roughly 100-150 m, and the whole kit vanishes with the tile at fog distance. Texture set per kit item is albedo + normal only, 1024², one shared atlas per material family (metal / concrete / plastic). Total kit texture footprint in ETS2 terms is single-digit MiB, not tens.

## Risks
- HIGHEST: physics. Guardrail colliders already run unconditionally (tileManager.js:2419-2429, outside any CONFIG gate) and the exact failure mode is documented - street-tree colliders at the curb made the car 'stop dead the moment it touched a sidewalk' (changelog.md:885) and had to be killed with ENABLE_TREE_COLLISION:false. Adding W-beam colliders to 141 km of at-grade shoulder without a per-family kill switch and an outboard-offset rule will reproduce that bug at ten times the scale.
- At-grade placement driven by terrain drop-off can strobe. DEM is bilinear-sampled and box-blurred (changelog.md:110-120); a naive 'terrain drops 1 m' test will flicker rails on and off across tile seams. The existing D3 above-terrain-frame test (gotchas.md:814) exists precisely because a previous version of this test false-triggered - it is marked 'do not re-flag'. Reuse it verbatim.
- Barcelona is not a German autobahn. Eixample is a dense urban grid with NO guardrails anywhere. The ETS2 W-beam look only reads correctly on Ronda de Dalt, Ronda Litoral, B-23 and the trench corridors. If placement scatters W-beam through the Eixample grid to satisfy 'railing on roads', it will look actively wrong and the user will reject it. The night-Eixample perf benchmark (MEMORY: optimize-for-night-mode) is therefore the WRONG place to measure this subsystem's value - budget it there, but demo it on the Ronda.
