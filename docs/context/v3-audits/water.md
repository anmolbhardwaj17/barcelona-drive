# v3 audit — Water: sea surface, harbour/marina basins, wet roads & rain
**VERDICT: REBUILD**
## Justification
Split by file, because the domain is not homogeneous — but the aggregate is REBUILD.

`coastline.js` is KEEP, unchanged. It is correct, measured-good work, and it already solves the hard geometric problem (real OSM shore, verified seaward closure, one-query sea/distance sampling). Nothing in the ETS2 target requires touching it.

`waterRenderer.js` is REBUILD, and this is not a sunk-cost or a code-aesthetics call. It is a lakes-and-ponds `ShapeGeometry` filler written for Delhi. It has never executed once in Barcelona (`ENABLE_WATER: false` since commit 809bd94). Two of its three feature paths are provably unreachable against the real data (0/426 tiles have piers; 8/426 have a populated `marinas[]` while 1400 marina polys sit in the array it does not read). Its output is an untextured, unanimated, `depthWrite:false` Lambert quad — architecturally incapable of the normal-mapped/fresnel/glint bar with any amount of patching, because it has no shader hook at all. Turning `ENABLE_WATER` on today would additionally make every tile try to build the entire region's 254 water features (see the region-global bake bug). The rebuild is small — roughly 150 lines that read closed water bodies out of `tileData.water[]`, merge one flat mesh per tile at a single sea-level Y, and share the SAME shader chunk as the terrain sea branch so harbour water and open sea match exactly. Keeping the old file and bolting a shader onto it costs more review time than deleting it, and produces a worse result because the marina-Y and pier assumptions are wrong at the data level, not the code level.

The sea SURFACE is REBUILD at the bake, not at the renderer. The decisive measurement is tile `16_33168_24478`: our sea is a 3.73 m-relief hillside floating 3.6 m above Y=0. Every previous attempt in this repo failed on that — the ocean plane got buried, marina meshes z-fought, sea tiles even produced NaN seam normals that rendered as a 2 km white wall (`changelog.md:1104`). No shader fixes a non-flat sea: a scrolling normal map on a tilted, lumpy surface reads as shimmering hillside, not water. So the first work item is bake-side: put `'coastline'` back into the sink pass in `buildRegion.js:1178-1240` and flatten open-sea cells to a fixed level with a short offshore ramp. Once the sea is genuinely flat at a known Y, everything downstream becomes cheap and safe.

The sea RENDERER, by contrast, is KEEP-and-extend, and I am deliberately NOT proposing a separate ocean mesh. Fog kills the sea past ~350 m and `GRID_RADIUS=1` means the visible sea is entirely inside already-loaded terrain tiles. Riding the existing terrain mesh costs 0 draws and 0 triangles; a camera-following sea plane would cost a draw and 8k tris to look identical. `terrainRenderer.js`'s `onBeforeCompile` already carries `vWorldPos`, `vCoast` and a `customProgramCacheKey` — a `if (vCoast > 0.5)` water branch drops straight in, and because the sea fills large contiguous screen regions the branch is warp-coherent. Rebuilding this into a separate system would be a rewrite for its own sake.

WET ROADS: YES, unambiguously worth it, and it is the highest value-per-day item in my whole domain. The user asked for ETS2 feel; ETS2's rain look is roughly half its identity. The unusual thing here is that we are already 70% wired for it and nobody noticed: `sharedRoadMaterial` is ALREADY `MeshStandardMaterial` with a roughness term (`roadRenderer.js:304`), `patchRoadWash()` already injects into every ground material (`roadRenderer.js:283`), and a PMREM sky cubemap already exists in VRAM for the car (`carModel.js:155`) and can be promoted to `scene.environment` at ~0 marginal cost. Wet roads therefore reduce to one `uWet` uniform, one procedural puddle mask, one ripple normal fetch, and an envMap assignment — 3 days, not 3 weeks. Critically it also DODGES the project's #1 unsolved problem: with zero punctual streetlights, most PBR work buys nothing at night, but wet asphalt is exactly the surface where the 2 existing headlight SpotLights produce the ETS2 money shot — a long specular streak stretching down the road ahead of the car. Wet roads are the one material upgrade that gets BETTER, not worse, from our degenerate night light rig. If the lighting domain later restores punctual lamps, this item's payoff triples for free.
## ETS2 reference
ETS2 (post-1.3x, and its Scandinavia/Iberia DLC coastlines) does the following, and this is the calibration target — not a photoreal ocean:

GEOMETRY: open water is a single low-tessellation horizontal quad/grid per water body, authored flat at a fixed water level. No displacement, no Gerstner waves, no tessellation, no LOD chain — one mesh, one draw, one material. Rivers/canals are ribbon strips along the spline. Water never has physics.

SHADING: one or two tiling normal maps (typically ~512-1024 px, DXT5nm / two-channel) scrolled in opposite directions at different UV scales — a slow large-scale "swell" layer and a faster fine "chop" layer — summed and renormalised. On top: a Schlick fresnel term that fades a flat deep-water albedo into the sky/cubemap colour at grazing angles, a Blinn-Phong sun-glint lobe (high exponent, blown out into bloom), a shallow-depth colour ramp toward the shore, and a scrolling foam band at the waterline driven by a depth/distance term. Reflection is a static/low-res environment cubemap, NOT planar or SSR. Refraction/depth-fade only in later versions.

WET ROADS (the standout, and the thing worth stealing): after rain ETS2 does NOT change geometry. It lerps the road material's specular/roughness toward mirror-smooth, darkens albedo ~25-30%, adds a puddle mask (a low-frequency tiling mask, weighted toward the road's camber gutters) which alone receives an animated ripple normal, and lets the cubemap + headlights + sodium lamp lights do the rest. Add falling-rain camera-locked streak particles and a windshield droplet overlay. That is the entire recipe. It costs one uniform, one mask, one extra normal fetch — and it is roughly half of ETS2's visual identity.

"Not too good but better than ours": their water still reads as a flat plane with moving noise, it has no proper wave silhouette, and the horizon joint is hidden by fog. We are one full tier BELOW that: our sea has no normal map, no animation, no fresnel, no specular, and is not even flat.

Two files, opposite verdicts, plus a third system that is where the sea actually lives.

3) THE SEA ITSELF IS TERRAIN VERTEX PAINT — `terrainRenderer.js:693-712`. Per-vertex RGB written into the terrain colour buffer, consumed by a `MeshLambertMaterial` (`terrainRenderer.js:770-776`), masked out of the procedural-green shader by the `aCoast` attribute (`:766`, `:882`, `:887`). Cost today: ZERO extra draws, ZERO extra triangles. Also zero normal map, zero animation, zero fresnel, zero specular, zero glint.

4) THE SEA IS NOT FLAT AND NOT AT SEA LEVEL. `backend/worldBuilder/buildRegion.js:1194` puts `'coastline'` in `OPEN_WATER_TYPES`, which are explicitly EXCLUDED from the water-sink pass — so open sea keeps its raw Copernicus GLO-30 surface value. Far-offshore tiles (≥~1.5 km out, DEM nodata) are exactly 0.00. This is the load-bearing defect: it is why the old ocean plane failed, why marina meshes z-fight, and why no normal-map/fresnel water shader can work here.

5) BAKE BUG, previously undocumented: `tileData.water[]` is REGION-GLOBAL, not per-tile. 280 of 426 tiles carry a byte-identical 254-feature water set (32 coastline segs + 181 water polys + 3 marina + 2 dock + 23 stream + 11 canal + 2 wetland); 142 tiles carry none; 4 carry a small distinct set. 6) WET ROADS / RAIN: do not exist. No weather system anywhere (every `grep -i rain` hit in `frontend/src` is the substring inside "terrain"). BUT the runway is already built: `roadRenderer.js:304` `sharedRoadMaterial` is ALREADY `MeshStandardMaterial` (roughness 0.9, metalness 0), and `patchRoadWash()` (`roadRenderer.js:283-300`) already `onBeforeCompile`-patches every road/sidewalk/panot material with a night uniform. A PMREM sky env map is already generated at `carModel.js:124-160` and used only by the car paint. 2 headlight SpotLights exist (`carModel.js:279`, day/night intensity swap at `:287-292`) and are the only punctual lights in the scene.

7) DEAD ASSET: `frontend/public/textures/water/sea_01.png` — 512x512, 8-bit palette PNG, 238 KB, Delhi-era, referenced by ZERO source files. Same class of finding as the unused card trees.

8) Fog is `FogExp2` density 0.005 (`scene.js:658`) and `GRID_RADIUS = 1` (`tileManager.js:57`). Effective sea visibility is a ~0-350 m band before it is fully fogged — which caps how much sea geometry is ever worth paying for.

## Risks
- THE BAKE SEA-SINK IS THE HIGHEST-RISK ITEM IN MY DOMAIN AND IT IS ALSO THE ONE EVERYTHING ELSE BLOCKS ON. It classifies grid cells against a stitched OPEN polyline — exactly the operation that produced gotcha G-13 (a coastline closePath filling a 25,000 m2 inland triangle over the Ronda Litoral tunnel approach) and G-14 (bufferPolyline self-intersection lobes). Mitigation: do NOT write a new classifier; port coastline.js:117-133's offshore-point PIP verification bake-side, and gate the commit on a validator that asserts no road-bearing cell falls below 0 m and no sea-classified cell rises above -0.5 m.
- RE-BAKE BLAST RADIUS. ~10-30 min, re-commits 547 MB, bumps TILE_VERSION in mapLoader.js and tileParserWorker.js, requires every live player to run window._clearTileCache(), and requires regenerating backend/tiles/barcelona/citymap.bin via backend/tools/buildCityMap.js (changelog.md:978 — it is gitignored, so the deploy must run the tool). It also touches BAKED_ROAD_ELEVATION_IS_RAW territory, which CLAUDE.md flags as breaking the running game if got wrong. Both my P0 items MUST ship in one re-bake, and it should be merged with whatever the terrain and roads domains need baked.
- CLIPPING water[] PER TILE BREAKS coastline.js's CURRENT ASSUMPTION. coastline.js:38 states 'the first processed tile hands them here' and ingestCoastline returns early once _osmShore is set — it works today only BECAUSE water[] is region-global (claim W3). Clipping without changing ingestCoastline to accumulate across tiles would leave the sea polygon defined by whatever fragment happened to be in the spawn tile, and the 3 km sanity floor at coastline.js:83 would silently reject it, falling back to the hand trace that already flooded Vila Olimpica by 300 m (changelog.md:1078). These two changes are coupled and must land together.
- WET-ROAD SPECULAR IS HOSTAGE TO THE NIGHT LIGHT RIG. With only 2 headlight SpotLights (carModel.js:279), the wet look is confined to the headlight cone. It still works and is still the best available night upgrade (claim W7), but if the lighting domain does NOT restore punctual streetlights, distant wet asphalt at night will read as darker-but-flat rather than glistening. I consider this acceptable; the reviewer should not be surprised by it.
- ADDING envMap TO ROAD MATERIALS TRIGGERS A FULL SHADER RECOMPILE of every road/sidewalk/panot program on first assignment in three r183. If it happens mid-drive it is a visible multi-frame stall. Must be routed through the existing boot warm-up at main.js:686 (which, ironically, currently warms the dead water material instead — see claim W12).
- PUDDLE NORMALS VS THE polygonOffset LAYER STACK. groundLayers.js:20-36 assigns 17 distinct depth biases from terrain (0) to stencil (-18). A puddle ripple normal that perturbs shading on the road layer must not visually fight the marking (-14), crossing (-16) and stencil (-18) paint drawn on top of it. Paint should stay dry-looking and matte or it will read as floating.
- SEA NORMAL PERTURBATION ON A LAMBERT MATERIAL ONLY WORKS IF THE SEA IS GENUINELY FLAT. There is no TBN on the terrain material (no normalMap defined), so I perturb in world space assuming the geometric normal is up. On today's 3.73 m-relief sea (claim W2) that produces shimmering hillside, not water. This is a hard block, not a degradation.
- MOBILE SCALE-DOWN. The binding constraint says scale down, not exclude. My fallback ladder: drop the second (chop) normal layer, drop the glint lobe, drop rain particles and the windshield overlay entirely, keep fresnel + a single scrolling normal. That degrades to roughly 0.4 ms and 0.68 MiB, and still looks better than today's flat paint.
- SCOPE HONESTY: the sea will still be a flat plane with moving noise. It will have no wave silhouette, no shoreline break, no boat wakes and no depth refraction. That IS the ETS2 bar and I am matching it deliberately, but nobody should read 'water rebuild' and expect an ocean simulation.
