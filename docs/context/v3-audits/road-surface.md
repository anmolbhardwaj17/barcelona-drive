# v3 audit — Road surface, markings, curbs, sidewalks (asphalt material, lane paint, wear, crosswalks, curbs, panot sidewalks, bike lanes, drain covers, ground Y-layering)
**VERDICT: REFACTOR**
## Justification
REFACTOR the module; REBUILD the material/shader layer inside it; DELETE four Delhi-era subsystems. Not a full rewrite, and I want to be explicit about why, because the no-slacking rule cuts both ways here.

The geometry pipeline is not a dead end — it is already the ETS2 approach. `buildFlatRibbonGeometry` (roadRenderer.js:499) emits exactly the strip topology SCS uses, and `roadBaker.js:400-401` already bakes the two attributes a proper road shader needs: world-metric arclength U and per-vertex `halfWidth`. That means correct world-metric asphalt UVs at any carriageway width are recoverable **in the vertex shader with no re-bake and no geometry change**. Junction clipping, width tapering, ramp divergence, miter joins and the terrain-conformance contract all work and represent hundreds of hours of debugging (see the 2026-07-16 Y-audit). A rewrite would spend 12+ days re-deriving that and land in the same place, while re-opening the road↔physics Y contract that the vertical-model spec locks. Sunk cost is not the argument — the argument is that the END RESULT of a rewrite is identical geometry with more risk.

What genuinely cannot reach the ETS2 bar no matter how much is patched is the MATERIAL STRATEGY, and that gets rebuilt, not patched: a per-vertex sine function (`roadNoise`, :317) sampled on 4-8 m spaced vertices can never produce texture, because it has no spatial frequency above ~0.125 cycles/m. No amount of tuning `br/bg/bb` fixes that. The whole asphalt/paint/kerb material layer is replaced with a KTX2 tiling albedo+normal + detail-tiling + analytic wear shader.

Why this domain deserves priority over every other art domain, with a number: the sun sits at 35° elevation (`scene.js:602`). On a HORIZONTAL surface, a ±20° normal perturbation swings N·L from sin(15°)=0.259 to sin(55°)=0.819 — a 3.2× luminance modulation. On a sun-facing VERTICAL facade the same perturbation swings cos(15°)=0.966 to cos(55°)=0.574 — 1.7× — and on the other three facade orientations it is ~0. Roads are therefore the highest-return normal-map surface in the entire project by a factor of 2-to-infinity given the existing light rig, and they need no new lights to pay off. At night, the two headlight SpotLights (`carModel.js:279`, 56° cone) point at nothing else; a normal map is the only thing that will stop the headlight pool being a flat ellipse on a flat plane. This domain is the one place where the structural "zero punctual lights" cap does NOT block the win.

The Y-layering ruling: REBUILD as a proper layer system — but half of it already exists and is right. `groundLayers.js` is a correct polygonOffset table. The defect is that the Y-lift system was never removed when it landed, so every paint surface is double-biased and physically floats 1.5-3 cm above the asphalt. Depth precision is not the excuse: DEPTH_COMPONENT24 with near=1 gives ~0.6 mm resolution at 100 m. Delete all seven paint-family Y constants, delete the hidden `+0.02` inside the ribbon builder, keep exactly two Y offsets (`ROAD_VISUAL_ABOVE_TERRAIN` — a genuine road-vs-terrain mesh-conformance gap, and `ROAD_PRIORITY_Y_BUMP` — an intra-material carriageway tie-break that polygonOffset structurally cannot express when all roads share one merged geometry), document both as load-bearing, and add a dev assert that fails any material setting polygonOffset outside `applyGroundLayer`.

DELETE, unconditionally, with a measured win: the edge-strip subsystem is 8,588-9,496 triangles per dense tile — twice the baked road surface — rendering an unbiased, mostly-buried 0.10 m dark-grey ribbon along every footway in the city, from a function whose sidewalk half is already `if (false)`. Across 25 resident tiles that is up to ~215 k triangles, 8% of the entire city triangle budget, spent on an artifact that is at best invisible and at worst the "broad black lines" its own author had to exclude motorways to hide. It is superseded by the baked granite kerb. Also delete `shoulderRenderer.js` (Delhi dirt shoulders; Barcelona has kerbs), `buildRoadsideBlendStrip` (hardcoded Delhi dust `#868840`), `buildBridgeShadowMesh` (already force-nulled at :4777), `decalRenderer.js` (Delhi wall posters, not a road system), and five dead material factories.
## ETS2 reference
ETS2 (Prism3D) road surfaces, at the "not too good but better than ours" calibration:

GEOMETRY. Roads are spline/prefab-driven quad strips, ~1 segment per 4-8 m, 2 verts per cross-section — structurally identical to our `buildFlatRibbonGeometry` (roadRenderer.js:499). They are NOT high-poly. Kerbs are modelled L/chamfer profiles at ~4-6 tris/m. Sidewalks are flat quads. So ETS2's road GEOMETRY budget is roughly what we already spend. The gap is entirely in the material.

MATERIALS. One tiling asphalt material per road class: DXT1/DXT5 diffuse + normal, typically 1024² (2048² only on hero motorway surfaces), tiling every ~4-8 m in both axes with WORLD-METRIC UVs (a 20 m carriageway gets 5 repeats across, a 6 m street gets 1.5 — never a normalized 0..1 stretch). A second "detail" sample of the same or a smaller (512²) normal at ~8× frequency breaks the repeat at bumper distance. Wear ruts, tar seams and patch repairs are painted into the 1K albedo+normal, plus per-spline vertex-alpha blending between a clean and a patched variant for macro variation.

PAINT. Lane paint is TEXELS, not geometry. Older SCS roads bake the full cross-section (shoulder, edge line, lane, centre line) into a strip texture mapped across V; newer ones use separate mapped overlay strips. Either way the paint shares the road's mip chain, so it has grain, chipped edges, tyre-polished centres, and it anti-aliases and fades correctly with distance instead of shimmering. Paint is co-planar with asphalt — never lifted in Y.

KERBS/SIDEWALKS. Tiling concrete/granite kerb texture + normal (512² is enough — the kerb is 0.12 m tall). Sidewalk paving 1024² albedo + normal, world-metric UV so the paving unit stays a fixed physical size.

LIGHTING RESPONSE. This is the payoff: on a horizontal surface a normal map directly modulates N·L. That's why ETS2 asphalt reads as a surface and not a plane, on integrated graphics, with a modest light rig.

LOD. 2-3 material tiers: near = albedo + normal + detail; mid = albedo + normal; far = albedo only. Distant road segments drop to a flat variant. Total road texture set for a whole region: on the order of 8-14 maps.

CALIBRATION CHECK: this is 1K tiling maps, one detail sample, analytic-or-strip paint, and a kerb texture. It is not photoscan, not 4K, not parallax-mapped, not SSR. It is reachable.
## Current state
ZERO road textures exist. `frontend/public/textures/` contains no asphalt, road, kerb or paving asset (verified by `find . -iname "*asphalt*" -o -iname "*road*"` → nothing). The entire road texture library is two Canvas2D procedural textures in `map/generate-road-atlas.js:161` — a 256² panot tile (`:14`) and a 128² bike pictogram (`:73`).

ASPHALT (`roadRenderer.js:302-315`): `MeshStandardMaterial({ color:0xffffff, vertexColors:true, roughness:0.9, metalness:0, side:DoubleSide })`. No map, no normalMap, no roughnessMap, no aoMap. All visual variation comes from `applyRoadVertexColors` (`:324-370`), a 3-octave sine `roadNoise` (`:317`) evaluated PER VERTEX on a mesh whose vertices are ~4-8 m apart — so the "oil stains and wear" are a smooth 8 m-wavelength gradient, not texture. Base colour is hardcoded `br=0.22, bg=0.25, bb=0.30`. UVs: `roadRenderer.js:592` and `backend/worldBuilder/roadBaker.js:400` both emit `uvs.push(u, 0, u, 1)` where `u = arclength / ROAD_UV_REPEAT_M(4)`. U is world-metric; **V is normalized 0..1 across the carriageway regardless of width**. A tiling texture applied to these UVs stretches 5× on a 20 m road vs a 4 m one. HOWEVER `halfWidth` is baked per-vertex (`roadBaker.js:401`, read at `roadRenderer.js:4626`) and survives the merge (`tileManager.js:141-168` keys buckets on attribute signature), so a world-metric V is recoverable in the vertex shader with NO re-bake: `vAcross = (uv.y-0.5)*2.0*halfWidth`.

MARKINGS (`roadRenderer.js:1180-1332`): geometry ribbons, 0.10 m wide, one BufferGeometry PER DASH (`:1125`), merged into one `MeshBasicMaterial({color:0x8a8a8a, vertexColors:true})` (`:1021`). ## Risks
- HARD DEPENDENCY, ORDERING RISK: there is no anti-aliasing anywhere (scene.js:526 antialias:false; no AA pass in main.js:143-175). Shipping high-frequency normal-mapped asphalt into a zero-AA forward pipeline will shimmer at 80 km/h and look WORSE than the current flat colour. An SMAA pass MUST land before the road art. If the post domain does not commit to it, this whole plan is mis-sequenced and I would rather ship the deletes (W4/W5/W7) and the Y-layering rebuild (W6) alone.
- V-FLIP: WebGL ignores UNPACK_FLIP_Y_WEBGL for compressed uploads, so every .ktx2 renders un-flipped against UVs authored for flipY CanvasTextures. The panot UVs (roadRenderer.js:1690-1694) and the baked sidewalk UVs were both authored against the current flipY canvas. This fails SILENTLY — the panot pattern is 4-fold symmetric so a flip is invisible on it, but the asphalt wear ruts and the paint strip are not. Needs a per-map validation pose in the screenshot set.
- COLOR SPACE: three reads the transfer function straight off the KTX2 DFD (KTX2Loader.js:1232) and verifyColorSpace early-returns for compressed textures — no warning if wrong. A normal map encoded sRGB will look subtly, unfixably wrong on the largest surface in the scene. Must be asserted in the encode script with a non-zero exit.
- Y-LAYERING REGRESSION: this stack has bitten before (changelog.md:1134 is a full session on it) and the failure mode is subtle — paint that looks fine parked and wrong at 80 km/h over a crest. Mitigate with a committed grazing-angle + bump screenshot pose set captured BEFORE the change, and a temporary ?debug=ylayers overlay. Medium risk, and it is the one P1 item I would not ship unverified.
- V10 RE-BAKE GATE (W13/W14): ~10-30 min, re-commits 547 MB, bumps TILE_VERSION in mapLoader.js AND tileParserWorker.js, and every existing player needs window._clearTileCache(). 17 of 426 tiles are still v7 and will have neither the surface-class byte nor an AO grid — the new road material MUST have a null path for both or those tiles render wrong. This re-bake window must be COORDINATED with every other domain that wants one (tree species NUM_TREE_VARIANTS, a finer AO grid, baked tangents) so the city is re-baked ONCE.
- MESHSTANDARD-ON-ROADS contradicts v2-plan-hardened §5 Tier 3.2 ('MeshStandard deferred out of v2') and gotcha G-28 ('use Lambert for textured ground-plane meshes'). I am arguing to keep it on asphalt ONLY, because it is already there (zero migration cost) and horizontal surfaces are the single place where sky-IBL specular reads. If that argument is rejected, the fallback is Lambert + normalMap + aoMap, which loses the grazing-angle sheen but keeps ~90% of the win. Either way this needs an explicit ADR before asset #1, not a silent decision.
- MOBILE: `grep -i quality frontend/src/config.js` returns nothing — there is no quality-tier mechanism in the entire frontend. 14 MiB of road textures is survivable on a 4 GB iPhone, but the city-wide 200 MiB on top of a ~700 MB heap with Safari killing tabs around 1-1.5 GB is not. Roads must ship a half-res manifest variant and a coarse-pointer flag that skips the normal maps and the detail sample. Cheap for this domain, but it does not exist to hook into yet.
- ANISOTROPY: setRendererAnisotropy (roadRenderer.js:192) reaches exactly one texture. If the new asphalt maps are not explicitly wired in, the road turns to grey mush at grazing angles and the entire texture investment is invisible at exactly the distance the player looks at most. One line, easy to miss, high blast radius.
- SHADER OWNERSHIP COLLISION: patchRoadWash (roadRenderer.js:283-301) already OWNS onBeforeCompile on every road material, injecting the night wash and the v9 AO. If the proposed materialRegistry chokepoint (v2 item 1.5) lands, it must CHAIN onBeforeCompile, not assign it — three's CSM.js:443 hard-assigns and would silently delete both the night wash and the baked AO from every road in the city.
- MEASUREMENT HONESTY: the 5.0 ms GPU ask rests on an inference, not a reading (claim R-17). W1 is 0.5 days and must run before any asset is sourced. If it shows the road stack is already at or above 5.0 ms, the material-tier LOD becomes mandatory rather than optional and the P2 estimate grows by 0.5 days.
- ART DIRECTION DRIFT: ambientCG asphalt is photoscan. Dropped in raw next to the 9 Kenney city cars (2,032-2,476 tris, one 3 KB WebP palette atlas, metallicFactor 0) it makes them read as toys. The re-grade step in W9 is not optional polish — it is the thing that keeps the whole world coherent, and it is free today and unrecoverable after 100 assets.
