# v3 audit — Sky, clouds, fog, weather, time-of-day, lighting rig
**VERDICT: REFACTOR**
## Justification
Three different answers for three parts, and honesty requires saying so rather than averaging them into a fake single word.

KEEP the atmosphere half on MERIT, not on sunk cost. The aerial-perspective fog (scene.js:666-711) does altitude thinning, distance desaturation, distance blue-shift and a sun in-scatter wedge. ETS2 does a keyframed colour plus linear start/end plus a horizon blend. We are ABOVE the target here. Rewriting it makes the end result worse, which is the only test that matters. Same for the analytic grade and the warm-key/cool-shadow rig.

REBUILD the night lighting, because there is nothing there to refactor. Night is six independent decal and emissive fakes standing in for lights that do not exist (streetlightRenderer.js:526, meshMaterializer.js:917, roadRenderer.js:273, vegetationRenderer.js:235). No amount of tuning them reaches ETS2's night, because what makes ETS2's night READ is view-dependent specular response on damp asphalt to real punctual lights, and a pre-multiplied-alpha decal lying on the ground is structurally incapable of producing a view-dependent highlight. This is the #1 unsolved problem in the project and it is a lighting-ARCHITECTURE problem, not an art problem -- no texture from any other domain fixes it. It is also more affordable than anyone has costed: the fakes it deletes are themselves expensive (one 16 m transparent quad per lamp at 22 m spacing blankets the visible road surface in 1-2 blended layers), and its data feed already exists unused -- tileManager.getStreetlightPositions() (tileManager.js:3482, exported :3523) has ZERO callers, left over from the deleted PointLight pool.

DELETE dayNight.js + timeSystem.js -- 186 lines of a second, contradictory lighting rig that double-adds ambient and hemisphere lights, replaces the exponential fog the aerial-perspective chunk is tuned for with linear fog, and whose only reason to exist (time of day) is better expressed as a parameterization of envToggle, which is the actual authority. The 2026 revert was not a verdict on time-of-day; it was a verdict on a rig that never went through the graded preset path.

Net: ~60% of the code stands unchanged, the night lighting is new architecture, 186 lines are deleted. Calling this a whole-domain REBUILD would be rewriting the one thing we are already good at, which is the opposite of the no-slacking rule.
## ETS2 reference
SCS Prism3D, calibrated at "not too good but better than ours". SKY: a per-climate sky CUBEMAP (DXT, historically 512^2/face, 1024^2 on newer climates) cross-faded across ~8 keyframed time-of-day slots, plus a sun/moon billboard with a corona flare and a star cube at night. Not an analytic gradient -- a texture, which is why their dawn/dusk skies have cloud structure and colour banding a 3-stop gradient cannot produce. CLOUDS: two scrolling layers -- a 1024^2 alpha cloud SHEET on the dome scrolled by a wind vector, plus a small set of billboarded cumulus. Never volumetric. FOG: per-climate keyframed fog_color + linear fog_start/fog_end + a horizon blend so distant geometry melts into the sky band, with a weather density multiplier. No aerial-perspective desaturation, no altitude thinning, no sun in-scatter wedge -- OUR fog model is strictly more sophisticated than theirs. LIGHTING (the part that matters): one CSM directional sun (3 cascades), a per-TOD ambient + hemispheric term, and REAL dynamic point/spot lights for street lamps and vehicle headlights, governed by a camera-local light BUDGET exposed to the player as a "Lights" quality slider; lamps beyond the budget fall back to emissive + corona billboard only. Headlights are spot lights with a projected COOKIE texture (~256-512^2 single-channel beam pattern with a flat low-beam cut-off) plus a cheap light-shaft billboard. Their night reads as LIT rather than TINTED because of exactly three things: (a) real punctual lights, (b) a specular streak on damp asphalt under those lights, (c) corona billboards on every lamp head. WEATHER: discrete states (clear/cloudy/rain) per climate with cross-fades; rain = a screen-space droplet/streak overlay + a wetness scalar that raises road specular and darkens albedo + wheel spray + wipers. TOD: continuous ~24-minute cycle from keyframed climate profiles. Every texture in that list is 512-1024^2 DXT. The bar is modest; the ARCHITECTURE (camera-local real lights + wetness) is the part we do not have.

## Assets needed
- Cumulus cloud cutout atlas -- 8-12 cells, 2048x1024, RGBA (albedo + alpha only; no normal, clouds are shaded by the existing vertical gradient in the fragment shader). Source: AI-generated in Draw Things on the Mac ('isolated cumulus cloud, black background, soft edges', then alpha-matted), OR cropped from a CC0 Poly Haven sky HDRI which gives real photographic cloud structure for free. Prefer Poly Haven -- do not AI-generate what exists as a real photo. ~0.6 MB ETC1S, 2.8 MiB VRAM. Replaces scene.js:29-129.
- Stratus/overcast sheet -- 1024x1024, tiling in U, RGBA (albedo + alpha). AI-generated with TILING MODE ON (untiled AI output is unusable as a scrolling sheet). Used as one extra dome band for the OVERCAST preset. ~0.12 MB, 1.33 MiB VRAM.
- Headlight beam cookie -- 512x512, single-channel R8 (mask only). BLENDER-AUTHORED: model a real low-beam reflector, render the projected pattern orthographically -- hot centre, flat asymmetric cut-off at the top, spread wings. This is the ETS2 signature and derived-from-albedo AI normals are irrelevant here because it is a pure intensity mask. ~0.04 MB, 0.35 MiB VRAM.
- Lamp corona / flare sheet -- 512x512, RGBA, 4 cells (corona, anamorphic streak, star, soft halo). Blender-authored or hand-painted; a radial gradient with chromatic fringing. ~0.08 MB, 0.35 MiB VRAM. Takes over bloom's job of selling a lamp as a light source.
- Rain streak atlas -- 512x512, RGBA, 4 columns of motion-blurred falling streaks. BLENDER-AUTHORED (particles rendered orthographically with motion blur) or generated procedurally to canvas at boot for zero download. Prefer procedural. ~0.09 MB if shipped.
- Windshield/camera droplet map -- 1024x1024, RG (normal.xy) + A (mask). BLENDER-AUTHORED: metaball droplets on a plane, bake the normal from the high-poly. This is the true-normal bake path the v2 doc calls for; do NOT derive it from albedo luminance -- droplets are all curvature and a fake normal reads as flat blobs. ~0.15 MB, 1.33 MiB VRAM.
- Milky Way band (P4, optional) -- 2048x1024 RGB, downsampled from the NASA/ESA Tycho all-sky panorama (public domain, no attribution required, never NC). ~0.35 MB. Nice-to-have only.
- Sky-gradient TOD LUT -- 256x8 RGBA16F. GENERATED AT BOOT from the preset keyframe table. Zero download, ~16 KiB VRAM.

## Risks
- SKY-21's 1.2-2.0 ms is an ESTIMATE, not a measurement, and the entire night plan rests on it. Mitigated by the 1-day spike (32 stub lights on the road material only) placed BEFORE the 5-day build. If the spike returns above 3.0 ms the design re-scopes to a 256 m window and 2 lights per cell before a line of production code is written.
- Shader-program compile storm. Adding one define to every lit material invalidates all 125 compiled programs at once, and first-appearance compiles were already measured at ~100 ms frames (which is why warmAllBuildingMaterials exists at meshMaterializer.js:965-984). The warm list MUST be extended in the same commit as the opt-in, and must cover the instanced and batched variants -- the v2 audit found today's warm meshes are plain THREE.Mesh, so no USE_INSTANCING or USE_BATCHING variant is ever warmed.
- Sequencing risk on the fake-night deletion. If clustered lighting ships while the pool decals, hero spills and washes are still live, night double-lights and looks measurably WORSE than today. This is an all-or-nothing commit that cannot be half-landed. Mitigated by capturing 3 committed night screenshot poses BEFORE any of it starts.
- Lambert has no specular slot, so a punctual light on a wall is a flat brightening rather than a light unless the Blinn lobe in SKY-22 works. If it does not, the wet-asphalt payoff evaporates and the MeshStandard question genuinely reopens at +3.0 ms, which does not fit the budget. Highest-consequence unknown after the ms estimate.
- Fixing the fog-density clobber (SKY-2) is a LOOK CHANGE, not a bugfix -- day fog thins by 36%. It needs screenshot approval, not a silent commit. Same for un-hiding the night sky dome (SKY-5).
- The time-of-day rebuild risks a second revert; the last one was reverted as 'the auto-cycle's day went grey' (changelog.md:32). Mitigated structurally by building TOD as keyframes OVER the existing preset table so DAY and NIGHT stay exactly reproducible as two clamped values -- a revert becomes a one-line clamp instead of losing 3 days.
- Mobile. 4 lights + 5 texture fetches per fragment is a real cost on a tiler, and there is no quality-tier system anywhere in the frontend to scale it down -- the only device branching is three copies of (pointer: coarse) for HUD layout. Binding constraint 1 says the tier must scale DOWN, not exclude; today it structurally cannot.
- Grid cell popping. A hard 8 m cell boundary with a 4-light cap can pop as the camera crosses cells. Mitigated by nearest-first slot ordering so the dropped light is always the weakest contributor, but it must be verified in MOTION at 80 km/h -- a stationary check cannot surface it.
- The pool-decal saving (SKY-10, 0.6-1.1 ms) is the return half of the budget swap and it is estimated from coverage, not measured. If it turns out to be 0.2 ms the net GPU spend goes from +1.65 to +2.5 ms and something in the domain has to give -- most likely the rain pass, which is P3 anyway.
- Everything in this domain is RE-BAKE FREE. No tile format change, no TILE_VERSION bump, no 547 MB re-commit, no window._clearTileCache() for existing players. I am explicitly REJECTING baked lamp lightmaps (which would need a re-bake) in favour of runtime clustering, because lamps must also light moving cars and pedestrians -- a static lightmap cannot, and that is precisely the thing that makes ETS2's night feel alive.
